## What to Actually Post (Without Overthinking It)

Look, I won't pretend there's one magic formula. But there are a few content types that consistently perform well for small business owners.

**Stories from the job.** What happened this week that taught you something? What did a client say that surprised you? Real moments from real work cut through the generic motivational posts that clog everyone's feed.

**Opinions on your industry.** You've got 5, 10, maybe 20 years of experience. Share your actual take on something. Not wishy-washy "it depends" stuff, your genuine view. People follow people who have a perspective.

**Practical tips your ideal client would find useful.** If you're a financial adviser, what's one thing most people misunderstand about income protection? Write that. Short, clear, useful.

**Client wins (with permission).** Not bragging, just evidence. "We helped a client do X, here's what we did differently." That kind of post works hard for you.

The problem isn't knowing what to post. It's doing it consistently. That's where most small business owners fall over.

## How to Measure Whether LinkedIn Is Actually Working for Your Business

Don't obsess over follower counts. Genuinely, they're a vanity metric.

Watch for these instead:

**Profile views.** Are more people clicking through to see who you are? That means your content is sparking curiosity.

**Connection requests from relevant people.** When the right people start reaching out, something is working.

**Inbound messages.** This is the one that pays the bills. "Hey, I've been following your posts and I'd love to chat" is the goal. It takes time, usually 2 to 3 months of consistent posting, but it happens.

**Offline mentions.** "I saw your LinkedIn post about X" at a networking event means your content is landing beyond the platform.

LinkedIn for small business isn't a tap you turn on and immediately get leads from. It's more like planting fruit trees. Annoying to wait for, genuinely worth it once they're producing.
