## The Profile Fixes That Make an Immediate Difference

Before you post a single thing, sort out your profile. Honestly, this is where most people skip ahead too fast.

**Your headline.** The default is your job title. Nobody cares about your job title, they care about what you do for them. Rewrite it as a value statement. "I help SMEs in Queensland stop bleeding cash on inefficient hiring" lands harder than "HR Consultant."

**Your About section.** Write it in first person. Conversational. Tell people what you do, who you help, and why you do it. Three paragraphs max. Include a call to action at the end. Simple.

**Your featured section.** Pin something useful there. A result you're proud of, a piece of content that explains your thinking, a link to your website. Most profiles leave this completely blank, which is a missed opportunity every single time someone lands on your page.

Get these three things right before worrying about anything else.

## LinkedIn Personal Branding Tips for Actually Consistent Posting

Here's the uncomfortable truth. One good post every six weeks does almost nothing. LinkedIn rewards consistency over brilliance.

You don't need to post daily. Three times a week is genuinely enough to build meaningful LinkedIn growth in Australia. But those three posts need to show up reliably, week after week, not in a burst when you're feeling motivated and then radio silence for a month.

What should you post? A decent mix looks something like this.

- Something educational. Share a thing you know that others in your industry don't realise yet.
- Something personal. Not oversharing, just human. A lesson from a recent project, a mistake you made and fixed.
- Something that sparks conversation. A take, an observation, a question worth answering.

Rotate through these and you'll cover the full range of what builds a real personal brand.

### What LinkedIn Content Australia Actually Performs Well

Short posts punch above their weight on LinkedIn. Under 150 words, strong opening line, one clear idea.

Long-form posts work too, but only when the story is genuinely interesting. Nobody reads a wall of text for average insight.

Carousels and documents get shared. Video gets reach. But text posts from a credible voice still quietly outperform most formats when the writing is sharp.

Avoid stock photos. Avoid generic quotes over sunset images. Both feel hollow and people scroll straight past them.

## Engaging Without Being Desperate About It

Commenting is underrated. Seriously.

Leave a thoughtful comment on five posts a day from people in your industry or your target audience. Not "great post!" but an actual observation, a follow-up question, a point that extends the conversation. Do this for a month and watch what happens to your profile views.

It works because LinkedIn's algorithm treats comments as signal. But more importantly, real humans read those comments and form opinions about you based on them.

Your LinkedIn personal brand isn't just what you publish. It's also how you show up in other people's conversations.
