## Start With Your Profile Before You Post Anything

Your LinkedIn profile is a landing page. Treat it like one.

The first thing to fix is your headline. Not your job title. Your job title tells people what you are. Your headline should tell them what you do for them and why it matters.

"Mortgage broker helping Sydney buyers stop renting and start owning" hits differently than "Senior Mortgage Broker at XYZ Finance."

Same goes for your About section. This is where most people paste their CV summary and call it a day. Instead, write it like you're talking to a potential client who just asked, "So what do you actually do?" Be specific. Be human. Use your own voice.

One more thing: your featured section. This is prime real estate. Put your best content there, a case study, a post that blew up, a lead magnet. Don't leave it empty.

## What to Actually Post for LinkedIn Personal Branding That Works

This is where people get stuck. What do I even post about?

Honestly, three content types do most of the heavy lifting for LinkedIn personal branding Australia:

**Your perspective on industry stuff.** Not just sharing a news article with "Interesting thoughts here." Actually telling people what you think about it and why. Opinions attract engagement. Fence-sitting gets ignored.

**Stories from your work.** A client situation (anonymised obviously), a mistake you made and what you learned, a decision you wrestled with. People remember stories. They forget bullet points.

**Practical tips your ideal client would find useful.** Not generic advice. Specific, actionable stuff that makes someone think, "This person actually knows their stuff."

According to LinkedIn's own data, posts with personal stories get roughly three times more comments than purely promotional content. So yeah, the "be human" advice isn't just fluff. It works.

## How Often Should You Post?

Three times a week is the sweet spot for most people building LinkedIn personal branding with serious intent. Enough to stay visible in the algorithm, not so much that you're scrambling for ideas and posting rubbish just to fill a quota.

Consistency beats frequency every single time. Posting every day for two weeks then disappearing for a month actually hurts you. The algorithm notices. More importantly, your audience notices.

If you're a business owner, tradie, financial adviser, real estate agent, or anyone running a practice where LinkedIn could be a genuine client source, this consistency thing is non-negotiable. LinkedIn growth Australia isn't about going viral. It's about showing up regularly enough that when someone needs what you offer, you're already in their head.

## The Comment Section Is Half the Game

Here's something most LinkedIn branding guides skip entirely.

Your posts are only half the equation. The other half is leaving thoughtful comments on other people's posts. Not "Great point!" Not a fire emoji. Actual responses that add something to the conversation.

Why? Because every comment you leave shows up in the feeds of that person's followers. It's free visibility. And if your comment is genuinely insightful, people click through to your profile.

Ten good comments a week can do as much for your LinkedIn presence Australia as a solid post.
