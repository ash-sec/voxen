## H2: LinkedIn Content in Australia That Actually Gets Traction

Here's something that surprises people. Long, thoughtful posts consistently outperform short ones on LinkedIn right now. We're talking 800 to 1,500 words in some cases.

Not because people suddenly love reading. Because LinkedIn's algorithm interprets dwell time as a quality signal. If someone stops scrolling and reads your whole post, that tells the platform your content is worth showing to more people.

According to LinkedIn's own data, posts with 1,900 to 3,000 characters get the highest reach on average. That's roughly 300 to 450 words. Longer than most people write, shorter than a blog post.

The sweet spot for LinkedIn content in Australia right now is what I'd call the "informed opinion" format. Take something happening in your industry, share your actual take on it, explain why you think that, and invite people to push back. Controversial? A little. Effective? Very.

A few formats that are working particularly well:

**Lessons from a real mistake you made.** People are drawn to honesty, and vulnerability done right builds trust faster than any trophy cabinet post.

**Contrarian takes on common industry advice.** If everyone in your field is saying X and you genuinely believe Y, say so. Explain your reasoning. Watch what happens.

**Behind-the-scenes process posts.** How do you actually do what you do? Walk people through it. Specificity is underrated.

## H2: LinkedIn Personal Branding in Australia: Stop Doing These Things

A few habits that are quietly killing your growth right now.

**Posting and ghosting.** You drop a post, get some comments, and don't reply to any of them for 48 hours. The algorithm notices this. So do the people who took the time to engage. Reply within the first hour if you can, even briefly.

**Only posting when you have something to sell.** Your audience will clock this faster than you think. If every third post is subtly (or not so subtly) promotional, people tune out. The rough guide is one promotional post for every five or six value-focused ones.

**Ignoring your profile headline.** Your headline is premium real estate and most people waste it on their job title. Tell people what you do and who you help. "Mortgage broker helping first home buyers in Melbourne navigate a confusing market" beats "Senior Mortgage Broker at XYZ Finance" every single time.

**Copying what works for American LinkedIn influencers.** Australian professional culture is different. We respond differently to self-promotion, we're more sceptical of certain formats, and frankly some of the stuff that performs well in the US reads a bit cringeworthy over here. Know your audience.

## H2: LinkedIn Content Creation in Australia: The Practical Weekly Framework

Here's what a sustainable LinkedIn content creation routine looks like for an Australian professional in 2026.

**Monday:** An opinion or insight post. Something happening in your industry, your take on it.

**Wednesday:** A story or case study. Real experience, specific details, clear lesson.

**Friday:** Something lighter. A question for your network, a reflection on the week, something human.

Three posts. That's your baseline. If you can add a fourth on Thursday, great, but three is enough to build genuine momentum over time.

Set aside 90 minutes on Sunday evening or Monday morning to draft the week's posts in one sitting. Batching content is massively more efficient than trying to think of something to write every single day.
