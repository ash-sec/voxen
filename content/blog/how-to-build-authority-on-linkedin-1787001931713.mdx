## How to Build Authority on LinkedIn: The Fundamentals

### Pick One Lane and Stay In It

The biggest mistake? Posting about everything. Your gym session, your industry thoughts, your holiday in Bali, a motivational quote you found somewhere.

Pick a topic. One. Maybe two if they're closely connected.

If you're a mortgage broker in Brisbane, post about lending. Interest rate shifts, what first home buyers get wrong, how to actually read a comparison rate. If you're a financial adviser in Melbourne, post about money behaviour, retirement planning mistakes, things your clients ask you every single week.

LinkedIn personal branding Australia is built on consistency of topic, not frequency of posting. Your audience needs to know what they're signing up for when they follow you.

### Have Actual Opinions

Sharing industry news without any commentary? That's not authority. That's a newsletter nobody asked for.

The posts that build real credibility are the ones where you say "here's what I actually think about this, and here's why." Even if some people disagree. Especially if some people disagree, honestly.

It doesn't have to be controversial. It just has to be specific. "This common approach to X is fine, but here's what works better in my experience" is infinitely more useful than "Great article shared here!"

### Show the Work, Not Just the Win

Case studies, lessons from client situations, mistakes you made three years ago and what you'd do differently. This stuff builds authority faster than anything.

People don't trust perfection. They trust someone who clearly knows what they're talking about because they've been in the trenches.

Share the messy middle occasionally. Not in a self-indulgent way, but in a "here's what actually happens in my industry and nobody talks about it" way. That's the content that gets screenshotted and shared.

## How to Build Authority on LinkedIn With Your Profile, Not Just Your Posts

People check your profile after they read a post they liked. If the profile is weak, you lose them.

A few things worth fixing right now:

**Your headline.** It should say who you help and how, not just your job title. "Senior Consultant at XYZ" tells nobody anything. "Helping Australian SMEs reduce their tax burden without the jargon" is actually useful.

**Your featured section.** Put something here. A lead magnet, a case study, a link to your best post ever. This is prime real estate and most profiles leave it completely empty.

**Your about section.** Write it in first person and make it sound like a human wrote it. Because a human should have. If it currently reads like a corporate bio from 2014, rewrite it this week.

Your profile is your landing page. Treat it like one.
