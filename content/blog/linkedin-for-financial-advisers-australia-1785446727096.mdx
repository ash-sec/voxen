A profile with no posts, a stock headshot and a job title that reads "Financial Adviser at XYZ Wealth" tells them almost nothing useful.

But a profile with regular, thoughtful content? That's a different story entirely. It shows your thinking. Your personality. Your approach to money, markets, superannuation, whatever it is you actually specialise in.

## What to Actually Post (Without Sounding Like a Compliance Document)

This is where most advisers get stuck. Honestly, fair enough. ASIC guidelines are a real consideration, and nobody wants to accidentally post something that reads like a product recommendation.

But here's the thing. You don't need to give specific advice to create valuable content. Not even close.

Some of the highest-performing LinkedIn content from financial professionals is stuff like:

- **Myth-busting posts.** "Three things people believe about superannuation that cost them money" lands every time.
- **Honest observations about client behaviour.** "The most common mistake I see people make when they get a pay rise" is relatable and safe.
- **Life stage content.** Posts aimed at people in their late 30s buying a second property, or business owners approaching retirement, speak directly to who you're trying to reach.
- **Behind the scenes.** What does a first meeting with you actually look like? People genuinely want to know.

LinkedIn content Australia that performs well is usually specific, personal and a little bit opinionated. Not generic. Not vague. Not written like a quarterly newsletter.

Short posts work. So do slightly longer ones where you tell a story. What doesn't work is a wall of text that reads like it was written by a committee.

## Building a LinkedIn Personal Brand as a Financial Adviser

Your LinkedIn personal brand isn't your logo or your firm's colour palette. It's the answer to this question: when someone reads your posts for three weeks, what do they think you stand for?

For financial advisers, the most powerful personal brands tend to be built around a specific niche or philosophy. The adviser who talks exclusively to small business owners. The one who's obsessed with behavioural finance and why smart people make dumb money decisions. The adviser helping women take control of their finances after divorce.

Niche content attracts niche clients. And niche clients are usually ideal clients.

LinkedIn personal branding Australia for financial advisers isn't about being famous or having thousands of followers. It's about being the obvious choice for a specific group of people when they finally decide to get their finances sorted.
