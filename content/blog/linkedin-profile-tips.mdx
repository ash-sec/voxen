## LinkedIn Profile Tips That Start at the Top (Where Most People Mess Up)

Your banner and headline are the first things anyone sees. Not your experience. Not your recommendations.

Most Australians leave the banner as the default blue gradient. That's like wearing a grey hoodie to a job interview. It's not offensive, it's just... forgettable.

Your headline, though? That's where the real damage happens. "Director at XYZ Pty Ltd" tells me nothing useful. Who do you help? What do they get from working with you? A stronger headline sounds more like "I help Melbourne mortgage brokers win more referrals through content that builds trust" than a job title followed by a company name.

Forty words. Make every single one count.

### Your LinkedIn Profile Photo Matters More Than You Think

Blurry selfie? Cropped from a group photo where you can still see someone's shoulder? Mate, no.

A clean, well-lit headshot where you actually look like yourself is non-negotiable. You don't need a professional photographer, though it helps. You need good natural light, a neutral background, and a face that looks like it belongs to a human who is approachable and trustworthy.

Simple stuff. But you'd be surprised how many profiles get this wrong.

## The About Section: Stop Writing in Third Person

Writing your About section like a Wikipedia article about yourself is a trap. "John Smith is a seasoned professional with over 15 years of experience..." Nobody wants to read that.

Write in first person. Talk to the reader. Tell them what you do, who you do it for, and why you actually care about the work. Then, and this is the bit people skip, tell them what to do next. A call to action at the end of your About section is so basic and yet most profiles just... trail off.

LinkedIn content Australia is full of profiles that have genuinely impressive people behind them but communicate zero personality. Don't be one of them.

Keep it conversational. Drop the corporate jargon. Pretend you're explaining what you do to someone at a barbecue.

## LinkedIn Profile Tips for Your Experience Section

A lot of professionals copy-paste their job descriptions in here. That's a missed opportunity.

Each role is a chance to tell a story. What did you actually do? What changed because you were there? Bullet points are fine, but try to frame them around outcomes rather than responsibilities. "Responsible for managing client relationships" is forgettable. "Grew a portfolio of 40 clients from cold introductions to long-term retainers over 18 months" is not.

Specificity is your mate here.

### Skills, Endorsements and the LinkedIn Algorithm

Here's a stat worth knowing: profiles with more than five skills listed get around 17 times more profile views than those with fewer. That number is wild, but it tracks. The LinkedIn algorithm rewards completeness.

Add your skills. Prioritise the ones that are most relevant to the work you want to be doing now, not just what you've done in the past. And yes, ask people you've worked with for endorsements. It's a bit awkward, but it works.
