You're not a content creator. You're running a business.

## What to Actually Post: The Content Mix That Works

Here's where most business owners fall into a trap. They either only post about their services (too salesy, nobody engages) or they post vague motivational fluff that gets a few likes from mates and generates zero actual business.

What works is a mix. Roughly speaking, aim for something like this:

**40% insights and opinions.** Your take on something happening in your industry. What you've noticed, what's changing, what you think is being done wrong. Controversial? Good. Boring consensus takes get ignored.

**30% stories from the work.** A client situation you navigated (anonymised obviously). A mistake you made and what you learned. The moment something clicked. Real stuff, specific stuff, not a listicle about "5 tips for success."

**30% direct value.** Practical advice, a framework you use, a question worth thinking about. Things people can actually use on Tuesday morning.

This mix is the backbone of solid LinkedIn content Australia-based business owners actually see results from.

### How Often Should Business Owners Post on LinkedIn?

Three times a week is the sweet spot for most people. That's enough to stay visible in the feed without burning out or running out of things to say.

Less than twice a week and the algorithm basically forgets you exist between posts. More than five times and you start running out of genuine things to say, which is when the quality drops and people start tuning out.

Consistency beats frequency every time. Showing up three times a week for six months will outperform posting every day for three weeks then disappearing.

### The Profile Has to Back Up the Content

Your content strategy will only work if your profile is doing its job too. That means a headline that says what you actually do for actual people, not your job title. A summary that reads like a human wrote it. Recent activity that shows you're active.

Content without a strong profile is like a great ad that links to a broken website.
