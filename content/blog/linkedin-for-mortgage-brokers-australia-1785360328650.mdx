## What Actually Works: LinkedIn Content Australia for Brokers

Posting random inspirational quotes isn't going to cut it. Neither is sharing Reserve Bank announcements with zero commentary.

What works is content that makes someone think, "yeah, this person actually gets it."

Some of the highest performing content formats for mortgage brokers on LinkedIn:

**Break down complex stuff simply.** Fixed vs variable. Serviceability buffers. How lenders actually assess self-employed income. If you can explain something in plain English that people have Googled twelve times and still don't understand, you become the expert in their mind instantly.

**Share real scenarios (anonymised obviously).** "Had a client this week who thought they couldn't borrow because of their HECS debt. Here's what actually happened." People love a story with a real outcome. It builds trust without you having to say "trust me."

**Talk about mistakes.** Not your clients' mistakes. Common misconceptions. Things people do that accidentally tank their borrowing capacity. This positions you as someone who's seen it all, because you have.

**Your opinion on rate moves and market shifts.** Not financial advice. Just your take. People want to know what their broker thinks, not just what the RBA said.

Consistency matters more than anything here. Three posts a week beats one brilliant post a month, every time.

## Optimising Your LinkedIn Profile Before You Post Anything

One more thing before you start posting. Your profile has to do some work first.

If your headline just says "Mortgage Broker at XYZ Finance" you're leaving a lot on the table. Think about who you help and what outcome you give them.

Something like: "Helping first home buyers and property investors in Brisbane navigate the lending maze, without the jargon."

That's a human headline. It tells someone in three seconds whether you're for them.

Your About section should feel like a conversation, not a CV. What led you to broking? Who do you love working with? What do you do differently? Write it like you'd explain it at a barbecue, not in a compliance document.

Add a recent photo. Fill out your experience. Get some recommendations from past clients or referral partners. The profile is your first impression, and people do check before they reach out.
